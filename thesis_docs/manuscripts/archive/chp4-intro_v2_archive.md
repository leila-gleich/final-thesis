# Chapter IV: Results

## Introduction & Chapter Architecture Roadmap

This chapter presents the empirical findings and predictive performance evaluations for modeling and forecasting airport passenger security screening throughput volatility ($\sigma_{\text{TSA}}$ and $CV_{\text{TSA}}$). Grounded in heavy-traffic queuing principles—wherein checkpoint queues and passenger waiting delays escalate quadratically with arrival volatility ($C_a^2$) rather than static passenger volumes—this investigation evaluates three candidate model paradigms against an empirical Baseline Control across a strictly quarantined 2025 out-of-sample holdout dataset. Overall, the empirical findings demonstrate that model superiority is governed by decisive operational trade-offs rather than uniform dominance: the dynamic hybrid approach delivers superior accuracy and rapid error recovery during Irregular Operations (IROPS), supervised machine learning maximizes computational efficiency during routine daily operations, and deterministic flight schedule convolution achieves unmatched zero-shot spatial generalizability across airfields. To systematically present this empirical investigation, Chapter IV is organized into four sequential sections:

* **Initial Exploratory Data Analysis (Section 4.1)**: Synthesizes the multi-source longitudinal repository (capturing 42.06 million conformed records across the candidate Top 25 U.S. commercial airfields from 2019 to 2025), establishes operational data hygiene protocols, delineates post-pandemic temporal recovery boundaries, and characterizes baseline distributional dynamics across passenger screening throughput and flight departure delays.
* **Data Filtering and Subset Selection (Section 4.2)**: Implements the sequential four-tier filtering pipeline (evaluating data integrity, connecting passenger hub dynamics, carrier checkpoint isolation, and geographic representation) to isolate an experimentally controlled cohort of nine airfields spanning 12 carrier-exclusive terminal complexes.
* **Model Development and Execution (Section 4.3)**: Details operational feature engineering, cross-validation architectures, and the execution parameters for the Baseline Control, Model 1 (Deterministic Flight Schedule Model), Model 2 (Supervised Machine Learning Model), and Model 3 (Dynamic Two-Stage Hybrid Model) applied to the 2025 holdout evaluation period.
* **Model Evaluation and Results (Section 4.4)**: Evaluates candidate model performance against the empirical Baseline Control across three core operational dimensions—robustness under nominal and routine conditions, resilience during irregular operations (IROPS), and zero-shot spatial generalizability—while validating the empirical hypotheses governing the values versus volatility paradigm.

### Alternative Draft: Continuous Academic Prose (Single-Paragraph Format)

This chapter presents the empirical findings and comparative performance evaluations for modeling the stochastic volatility of airport passenger screening throughput ($\sigma_{\text{TSA}}$ and $CV_{\text{TSA}}$). Grounded in Kingman’s heavy-traffic queuing formula, which demonstrates that checkpoint delay backlogs escalate with passenger arrival burstiness ($C_a^2$), this study evaluates three candidate operational models—Model 1 (Deterministic Flight Schedule Model), Model 2 (Supervised Machine Learning Model), and Model 3 (Dynamic Two-Stage Hybrid Model)—against an empirical Baseline Control across a 2025 holdout dataset. The empirical results reveal that no single forecasting architecture is universally superior across all operational regimes. Instead, models exhibit distinct asymmetric trade-offs: Model 3 achieves decisive error recovery and resilience during Irregular Operations (IROPS), Model 2 provides optimal Pareto efficiency under routine daily operations, and Model 1 provides unmatched zero-shot spatial generalizability across airfields without local retraining. Furthermore, empirical tests validate the values versus volatility paradigm, confirming that checkpoint throughput volatility is coupled with flight departure delay variance (*r* = +0.4373, *p* < .05) rather than static volume or raw delay levels. To present these results systematically, this chapter is organized into four sections: Section 4.1 presents the initial exploratory data analysis across the longitudinal multi-source warehouse; Section 4.2 details the four-tier filtering pipeline that established the nine-airport, twelve-complex experimental cohort; Section 4.3 outlines feature engineering, training configurations, and 2025 holdout execution protocols; and Section 4.4 reports the formal comparative model evaluations across robustness, resilience, and generalizability.
