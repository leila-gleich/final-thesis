----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 SECTION PURPOSE & METHODOLOGICAL ROLE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This section provides the comprehensive descriptive statistics for the conformed and filtered 
experimental dataset produced by the 4-tiered purposive filtering pipeline (Section 2) and 
engineered according to the feature deconvolution framework (Section 2).

Unlike Section 1—which documented the macro-level census of the raw upstream feeds across 2019–2025—
this section characterizes the exact modeling cohort used for training, hyperparameter validation, 
and out-of-time holdout testing:
- Temporal Scope: Candidate B Post-Mask Mandate Regime (May 1, 2022 to December 31, 2025; 44 continuous months).
- Spatial Scope: 9 Purposively Selected Airfields (BOS, DFW, DTW, EWR, IAH, LAX, LGA, ORD, PHL).
- Total Analytical Sample: Exactly 270,460 conformed hourly observations.

Documenting these descriptive statistics *prior* to reporting model execution outputs (Section 5) 
is essential for establishing the baseline operational conditions, variance structures, and 
distributional properties across the temporal partitions.

Key Distributional Insights:
1. Variance Smoothing in Convolved Demand:
   While mean flight volume is identical (~20.9 flights/hr), the standard deviation of convolved 
   flights is significantly tighter (16.20 vs. 18.29), and the interquartile range is more continuous. 
   The lognormal arrival kernel smooths discrete flight pushbacks into realistic passenger flow.
2. Positive Skewness in Congestion Signals:
   Prior-hour flight delays exhibit high positive skewness (Mean = 12.08 min vs. Median = 4.11 min), 
   with severe right-tail extremes (Max = 1,319.50 min) reflecting major convective weather ground stops.
3. Checkpoint Passenger Volume Scale:
   The 75th percentile of hourly checkpoint demand reaches 3,494 passengers/hr, with peak holiday surges 
   reaching 9,569 passengers/hr across dedicated terminal screening complexes.

1. Post-Pandemic Secular Volume Expansion:
   Mean hourly checkpoint throughput expands steadily from 2,230.5 pax/hr (2022–2023) to 2,407.1 pax/hr 
   (2024) and 2,565.7 pax/hr (2025), reflecting a +15.0% cumulative post-pandemic demand recovery.
2. Stationarity of Operational Shock Frequency:
   In contrast to volume growth, disruption indicators remain remarkably stationary across all three folds:

   - Mean delay: 12.14 min (Train) -> 11.85 min (Val) -> 12.22 min (Test).
   This confirms that testing models on the 2025 holdout evaluates performance under real, unattenuated 
   operational shock dynamics.

3.5 CONDITIONED DATA CENSUS (ROUTINE VS. DISRUPTION SUBSETS)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To prepare for empirical model execution and evaluation in Section 5, the filtered dataset is 
categorized into conditioned subsets:

1. Routine Operational Windows (Nominal Hours):
   - Definition: PriorHourAvgDepDel < 15.0 minutes AND PriorHourCancelledFlights = 0.
   - Total Observations: 211,248 hours (78.1% of total sample).
   - 2025 Test Holdout Subset: 56,128 hours.
   - Mean Checkpoint Throughput: 2,412.3 pax/hr (Std = 1,682.4 pax/hr).

2. Acute Disruption Windows (Operational Shocks):
   - Definition: PriorHourAvgDepDel >= 45.0 minutes OR PriorHourCancelledFlights >= 5.
   - Total Observations: 18,492 hours (6.8% of total sample).
   - 2025 Test Holdout Subset: 4,928 hours.
   - Mean Checkpoint Throughput: 2,748.9 pax/hr (Std = 2,014.2 pax/hr).
   - Insight: Disruption windows exhibit a +13.9% higher mean throughput, reflecting delayed passenger 
     queue accumulation from held flights.
